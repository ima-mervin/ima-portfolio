Overview

Built for the hypothetical carrier OptiFlow Freight Solutions, this project models the long-haul route between San Francisco and New York for Tesla Semi trucks as a network optimization problem. It runs in two sequential phases: Phase 1 establishes a baseline shortest-distance path, and Phase 2 re-optimizes for minimum total operating cost under realistic operational constraints.

Core concepts

  • Shortest-path formulation — binary arc-selection variables with flow-conservation constraints across a network of major U.S. cities (Phase 1)
  • Minimum-cost routing — objective extended to fixed travel costs plus labor, with driver-team assignment, familiarity speed bonuses, a 60 mph cap, and strict driver-hour and 80-hour trip limits (Phase 2)
  • Guaranteed optimum — solved in Gurobi to a 0.00 optimality gap, confirming the global minimum
  • Synthetic data pipeline — 17 route segments generated with Mockaroo, distances estimated via Gemini and validated against Google Maps

Results

Phase 1 produced a shortest route of 49.73 driving hours. Phase 2, optimizing for cost, returned a minimum total cost of $5,104.38 over a 55.41-hour trip — comfortably within the 80-hour window — with a 0.00 optimality gap confirming a guaranteed global optimum. The cost-optimal path ran SF → Las Vegas → Albuquerque → St. Louis → Pittsburgh → New York.

# Phase 2 objective: minimize total operating cost
model.setObjective(
    gp.quicksum(
        (fixed_cost[i,j] + labor_cost[i,j]) * x[i,j]
        for i, j in arcs
    ),
    GRB.MINIMIZE
)

"Once cost, energy, and operating limits are in the model, the shortest path is not always the cheapest — labor allocation matters as much as route choice."

The analysis showed driver-hour limits were the most binding constraint, and that strategically swapping driver teams across western and eastern segments minimized total cost. Documented next steps include variable-speed charging, traffic and weather uncertainty, and a broader city network.
